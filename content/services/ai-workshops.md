## Who the workshop is for

For anyone who feels their team could get more out of AI, wherever you are right now:

- You are just starting and want to settle the basics and bring the whole team along.
- Some people use AI every day, others hardly at all, and you want everyone on the same page.
- You already work with agents and want them to run on their own, from ticket to pull request.

Developers and tech leads are just as welcome as product owners, QA and anyone who writes tickets or reviews results.

## Formats {#formats}

### Getting started: half a day to a full day

For teams that are just starting or where everyone works differently. We clarify which tools and data are allowed, bring everyone to the same level and complete the first tasks together with agents. Afterwards the team has shared rules and knows where AI pays off for you.

### Team workshop: one to two days on your own repository

The core format. First an audit of team, workflows and code, then we set up the agents and work on tickets from your backlog. Afterwards your repository has an `AGENTS.md`, configured agents and the first completed tasks.

### Ongoing support: several weeks

For teams that already work with agents and want to go further. We build agents with you that run on their own, for example for reviews in the pipeline, analysing bugs and user feedback, or preparing tickets. Plus regular sessions where we adjust whatever does not hold up in day-to-day work.

## Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or Copilot?

The tools are closer to each other than the marketing suggests. What matters more than the choice is that your repository and processes are set up for working with agents. Roughly: Copilot is often already there because it comes with GitHub or Microsoft licences. Cursor is now mainly an interface for agents: several agents run in parallel, locally or in the cloud, and the team reviews their changes in one place. Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode come from the terminal and suit larger, self-contained tasks.

Tools and models change constantly. Separating what is useful from marketing is exhausting. Once we know how your team works, we tell you what is worth it for your stack and your security requirements.

## Data protection and the EU AI Act

Before we start, we clarify what we may see and which data may go into which tool. If your data protection officer wants to weigh in, we involve them early. The rules we agree on together stay in the repository as a document.

Since February 2025, Article 4 of the EU AI Act has required companies to build AI literacy among their staff. Since August 2026, authorities have been checking this. A certificate is not required, but Germany’s Federal Network Agency (Bundesnetzagentur) recommends documenting the measures. We record the workshop’s content and participants in writing for that purpose.

## How a workshop runs

### Audit

We review team, workflows and code and work out what is costing time and money. That becomes the plan for the workshop.

### Workshop

We set up the agents with you and work on tasks from your backlog, over one or two days or in several shorter sessions. You do not get a long PDF full of jargon and tool names. We put the suggestions from the audit into practice directly with your team.

### Follow-up

After three to four weeks of day-to-day use we meet again, answer the questions that came up in real work and adjust whatever does not hold up.
